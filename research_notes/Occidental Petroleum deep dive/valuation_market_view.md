A bear variant is that a US-Iran deal collapses the front end toward the back end, or below it if OPEC+ spare capacity floods back.

## 3. Consensus bull/bear theses, ownership, Berkshire

### Takeaway
The consensus view is "high-quality, deleveraging oil beta that is still capital-return-constrained". Bulls point to record FCF, debt down to its lowest since 2019, Berkshire's support, midstream earnings and low-decline assets. Bears point to the preferred and debt claims ahead of buybacks, a modest dividend, collars, international and GoA disruptions, the 2027 unhedged downside on a backwardated curve, and carbon-capture capex. Berkshire has neither bought nor sold common since mid-2025.

## 5. Sum-of-the-parts: WES, OxyChem, low carbon

### Takeaway
OxyChem has been monetized ($9.5B adjusted, closed 2026-01-02, mostly used for debt). Stratos (1PointFive) DAC was delayed in commissioning, with startup reportedly slipping toward 2027. No analyst SOTP or WES stake valuation from 2026 was found.

### Cited Findings
- The OxyChem sale to Berkshire was agreed in October 2025 at an adjusted $9.5B and closed on 2026-01-02. Proceeds funded most of the H1 2026 debt retirement — [mexc](https://www.mexc.com/learn/article/occidental-after-the-oxychem-sale-what-changed-for-oxy-stock-in-2026-/1)
- Stratos was targeted for Q2 2026 startup but hit an unexpected "non-process component" delay in Phase 1 commissioning, with no revised date. ### Inferences: candidate variant perceptions

**Bull variants (the market may be missing):**
1. **Deleveraging is nearly complete at about $97 oil.** Net principal debt was about $7.6B (Q2). At Q2-like FCF of about $3B per quarter, the $10B gross target could be hit around Q3 or Q4 2026, unlocking the preferred-redemption cash build and an earlier buyback pivot than the "Hold" consensus assumes. Test: Q3 debt figure.
2. **Hedges roll off in 2027.** The $55–75.89 collars expire in December 2026, so 2027 cash flow could beat models that annualize hedged 2026 realizations, even at an $80–84 strip.
3. **Lower sustaining capex (−$900M) and a low-decline EOR base** are underappreciated against shale peers with steeper declines, supporting a higher multiple than its 11x P/FCF.
4. **Preferred redemption is accretive:** retiring an 8% pref cuts about $0.66B per year in dividends (my calculation, assuming the historical 8% coupon; verify).

**Bear variants:**
1. **Spot-to-strip collapse.** If a US-Iran deal reopens Hormuz fully, the front end could converge to the $80 back end or below, as happened after the mid-April ceasefire when OXY fell about 10%. OPEC+ members pumping below quota implies large latent supply. Price thresholds: WTI below about $70 would likely stall debt progress, and below $60 (the old collar floor area) would revive leverage concerns. These thresholds are my judgment.
2. **Non-recurring 2026 earnings:** record midstream and marketing earnings plus war-elevated realizations flatter the trailing FCF yield (21% on one measure), while the forward FCF yield on the strip is much lower.
3. **Middle East and GoA operating exposure** (international decline, GoA curtailments) differentiates OXY negatively from pure-play Permian peers in a Gulf war.
4. **Berkshire is not adding.** Q2 shares were unchanged even near the lows. Any Q3 13F sale would break a core pillar of the bull narrative.
5. **Capital allocation:** buybacks are subordinated to the preferred until about 2029, giving the lowest cash-return yield among peers.

**Data points that would invalidate a bull thesis:**
- Q3 or Q4 2026 principal debt not at or near $10B despite about $100 oil
- 2027 guidance with higher capex or flat or declining Permian volumes, or Permian below 795 Mboe/d
- Dec-2027 WTI below about $70
- A Berkshire 13F reduction
- No buyback framework or preferred plan by the Q4 2026 call
- Stratos capex overrun beyond the stated $5.5–5.9B range
