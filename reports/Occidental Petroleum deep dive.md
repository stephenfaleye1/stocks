# Occidental fixed its debt; now it must prove the payoff

As of 2026-11-03, Occidental Petroleum (OXY, $64.00 close on 2026-10-30, about $64B market cap) has largely finished its balance-sheet repair. The filings show principal debt down from about $20.8B in mid-December 2025 to **$11.8B at 6/30/2026**, net principal debt of **$7.6B**, no bond maturities of note before 2029, and interest expense running at roughly **$450M a year**, against $1,079M in 2025. The OxyChem sale to Berkshire Hathaway paid for most of this. What remains is a capital structure whose most expensive claim is no longer the bonds. It is Berkshire's **~$8.5B 8% preferred**, which costs about $680M a year and can be called only from August 2029, at 105%. It sits alongside about $5B of tail liabilities that OXY kept: a ~$2.3B Anadarko tax case, plus the Diamond Alkali/Passaic environmental exposure it retained when it sold OxyChem. Management has kept its balance-sheet promises early and repeatedly. It has missed every carbon-capture timeline, and the new CEO, Richard Jackson (in the role since 2026-06-01), is quietly shrinking that bet. **My stance is constructive but conditional: a modest buy below about $65.** The Street's Hold rating (target ~$67) prices OXY roughly as if long-run WTI were $70. Two things support upside: about $2B of self-help by 2027 (interest savings, low-carbon capex rolling off, hedges expiring), and a capital-return pivot that can arrive before 2029 if the warrants and cash build fund the preferred redemption. Both depend on long-dated WTI holding above about $70. Both can be checked against hard thresholds, starting with the Q3 2026 results due after the close on **2026-11-09**.

## Filings show a flat-volume upstream company earning wartime prices

The FY2025 10-K and the 2026 quarterly releases describe a company that has become an upstream-plus-midstream business and has stopped growing volumes. Production has beaten guidance every quarter reported in 2026. Q1 came in at **1,426 Mboed** ([Q1 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026030567/oxyex9913-31x26earningsrel.htm)) and Q2 at **1,433 Mboed**, 23 Mboed above the midpoint, with the Permian at 804 Mboed, about 56% of the total ([Yahoo Q2 highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-petroleum-q2-earnings-call-000352360.html)). Full-year guidance was narrowed to 1,423–1,453 Mboed with the midpoint unchanged, against a Q4 2025 run rate of about 1,481 Mboed ([4Q25 release](https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/investors/quarterly-earnings/oxy4q25earningspressrelease.pdf)). Management expects flat spending and flat output again in 2027 ([BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/08/07/occidental-sees-flat-spending-output-in-2027-keeps-focus-on-debt-reduction/)). This is a maintenance-plus posture, and the investment case has to rest on per-share cash flow rather than growth.

The October 13 pre-earnings 8-K is the only Q3 2026 data available, and it contains the first operational blemish of the year. Gulf of America facilities were curtailed in September by a third-party gas pipeline constraint and maintenance. Q3 GoA volumes were **~110 Mboed against a full-year guide of 132–136 Mboed**. Total production still landed "within guidance" of 1,400–1,440 Mboed ([Q3 pre-earnings 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026065586/oxyex99193026preearningsre.htm)). Domestic LOE rose to about **$8.54/boe from $7.80** in Q2. That is most likely GoA fixed costs spread over curtailed barrels, though this is an inference. Realized oil was $85.91/bbl, and crude collar settlements took **$94M** out of cash flow after a $156M drag in Q2 ([minichart](https://www.minichart.com.sg/2026/10/13/occidental-q3-2026-production-in-line-94m-collar-hit-gulf-curbs/)). An aggregator reports the collar terms as 100 Mbbl/d from March to December 2026, with a $55 floor and a $75.89 ceiling ([mexc](https://www.mexc.com/learn/article/is-oxy-stock-a-buy-in-2026-bull-case-bear-case-and-key-catalysts/1)). My arithmetic supports those terms: 100 Mbbl/d × 92 days × ~$10 above the cap is about $92M, and 91 days × ~$17 is about $155M. Both are close to the reported settlements. The collars expire in December, and the notes found no evidence of 2027 hedges.

Cash generation in 2026 has been exceptional, and much of it is due to the war. Q2 free cash flow before working capital was about **$3.0B**, the highest since Q3 2022. Adjusted EPS was $2.40 against a consensus of ~$1.86–1.92, and Midstream & Marketing posted a record ~$960M ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-q2-earnings-beat-oil-151000241.html)). Realized worldwide crude was $96.78/bbl that quarter, while Brent and WTI traded near $100 for much of the year on Iran and Strait of Hormuz disruption. Domestic gas realized *negative* $1.48/Mcf in Q2 because of the Waha dislocation ([Yahoo Q2](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-petroleum-corp-oxy-q2-010217270.html)), so OXY is effectively an oil-only commodity bet. Capex guidance of $5.5–5.9B was held in August. The 2027 capital starting point is $5.9B, with $700–800M of expected cash-flow improvement versus 2026 ([Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-occidental-beats-q2-2026-estimates-as-cash-flow-surges-93CH-4843829)).

The reserve base backs the flat-production story without giving it much room. YE2025 proved reserves were **~4.6 Bboe**. All-in reserve replacement was 98%, organic replacement 107%, and PUDs fell from 1,421 to 1,309 MMboe, 69% of them in the Permian ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026009059/oxy-20251231.htm); [4Q25 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026009054/oxyex99112-31x25earningsre.htm)). That works out to about 8.7 years of proved reserve life on my arithmetic. Bulls point to a ~16.5 Bboe total resource base ([bearstone Substack](https://bearstone2023.substack.com/p/occidental-petroleum-the-permian)), but the notes found no third-party Permian inventory ranking to test that claim. One more point from the filings matters for the risk profile. Selling OxyChem removed a chemicals cash flow that used to steady earnings across the oil cycle. OXY's commodity beta is therefore higher in 2027 than it was in 2024, just as its hedges expire.

## The Berkshire preferred is now OXY's costliest claim

### Debt is no longer the binding constraint

Deleveraging has been fast and front-loaded. The post-CrownRock pledge to repay $4.5B was met in Q4 2024, seven months early ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/02/18/3028338/0/en/Occidental-Announces-Further-Progress-on-Debt-Reduction.html)). Once OxyChem closed, principal debt fell $5.8B to $15.0B by February 2026 ([4Q25 release](https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/investors/quarterly-earnings/oxy4q25earningspressrelease.pdf)). It was $13.3B after Q1 and **$11.8B at 6/30/2026**, the lowest since Q2 2019, with **$4.2B of unrestricted cash** ([Q2 2026 release](https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/news-releases/pr-080526_occidental-announces-2nd-quarter-results.pdf)). Repayments in H1 2026 totaled about $8.6B ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026053388/oxy-20260630.htm)), more than the $6.5B of OxyChem proceeds originally earmarked. The stated next milestone is **~$10.0B** of principal. OXY needs $1.8B more to get there, and cash on hand already covers it.

The maturity wall is gone. At 3/31/2026 the 10-Q showed **nothing due in 2026, $48M in 2027, $14M in 2028**, and about $13.3B after that. Substantially all debt is fixed-rate ([Q1 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026030584/oxy-20260331.htm)). Notable remaining issues include the 8.875% notes due 2030 ($1.0B), the 5.200% notes due 2029 ($1.2B) and the 8.45% notes due 2029. The CrownRock tranches run out to 2054. A February–March 2026 tender, upsized from $700M to $1.2B, retired part of the Anadarko-legacy zero-coupon notes due 2036 at about 58% of accreted face, along with 2029–2031 paper ([OXY PR 2026-03-05](https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/news-releases/pr-030526_occidental-announces-total-consideration-for-its-cash-tender-offers-and-consent-solicitations-for-certain-of-its-senior-notes-and-debentures.pdf)). How much of the 2036 zeros remains, and the dates of the holder put that one note describes, were not verified. That put is the one contingent liquidity call left in the bond stack. Balance-sheet long-term debt of $13.54B sits above the $11.8B principal, most likely because of accretion and purchase-accounting adjustments.

Interest cost has collapsed. Interest and debt expense was $1,169M in 2024 and $1,079M in 2025, then fell to **$108M in Q2 2026**, down 60% year on year ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-q2-earnings-beat-oil-151000241.html)). Jackson said repayment "lowers our go-forward annualized interest by approximately $630 million compared to 2025" ([Yahoo Q2 highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-petroleum-q2-earnings-call-000352360.html)). That implies a run rate of about $450M, consistent with Q2 × 4. The ratings are split. Moody's has OXY at Baa3 with a positive outlook since April 2026, Fitch upgraded it to BBB in February 2026, and S&P rates it **BB+ with a positive outlook since May 2026** ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026053388/oxy-20260630.htm); [Cbonds](https://cbonds.com/news/3913763/)). An S&P move to BBB- would make OXY investment grade at all three agencies. Reaching $10B is the obvious trigger for that.

### Berkshire's preferred and warrants set the pace of shareholder returns

Of the original $10B 8% cumulative perpetual preferred issued for the Anadarko deal, **about $8.5B of liquidation value remains**. It pays 8% and becomes redeemable at OXY's option from 2029 at **105%**, not the 110% sometimes repeated ([Berkshire Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/2ndqtr26.pdf)). On my arithmetic, the coupon costs about **$680M a year, roughly 1.5 times the ~$450M of all remaining bond interest**, and a full call would cost about $8.9B. A cash-rich company is now paying 8% on a junior claim while its bonds yield far less. That ranks retiring the preferred above buybacks on pure cost-of-capital grounds, and management says the same. The order is debt to $10B, then a cash build alongside some further repayment ahead of the August 2029 call, with buybacks only "opportunistic" until then ([Yahoo Q2 highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-petroleum-q2-earnings-call-000352360.html); [gloom.sh transcript](https://gloom.sh/stocks/oxy/transcripts/q2-2026)).

The preferred also contains a mechanism that caps buybacks before 2029. Whenever trailing-twelve-month common distributions exceed **$4.00 per share**, OXY must redeem preferred equal to the excess. That forced about $1.5B of redemptions in 2023, and nothing has been redeemed since ([OXY ARS FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000079746825000056/pro013249_12xoccidentalpet.pdf)). Because the common dividend has never come close to $4.00, the 2023 trigger must have been driven by buybacks. That is my inference, and the notes did not confirm it directly. At about 1,012.8M diluted shares and the current $1.12 annual dividend, the threshold works out to roughly $4.05B of annual distributions. That leaves about **$2.9B a year of buyback room** before every extra dollar of buybacks is matched by a dollar of preferred redemption.

The warrants are the piece most commentary overlooks. Berkshire holds warrants on up to **83.9M shares at $59.59** ([Berkshire Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/2ndqtr26.pdf)). There are also 17.9M public warrants struck at $22.00 ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026053388/oxy-20260630.htm)). Both are in the money at $64. If Berkshire exercised, OXY would receive about **$5.0B in cash**, enough to fund more than half the preferred call. Dilution would be about 8%, and Berkshire's common stake would rise from about 26–27.5% to roughly 32% on my arithmetic. The diluted share count may already include part of this under the treasury method, so the incremental dilution is overstated if anything. The notes did not establish the warrant expiry terms.

### Contingent liabilities total low-single-digit billions, with fat tails

OXY sold OxyChem's plants but **kept its legacy tort and environmental liabilities**. They sit in a subsidiary, Environmental Resource Holdings, with remediation run by Glenn Springs Holdings "over many years" ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026009059/oxy-20251231.htm)). OXY also guarantees OxyChem's indemnification obligations to Berkshire ([Q1 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000162828026030584/oxy-20260331.htm)). The latest reserve figures the research found date from 2023: about **$1.03B accrued across 161 sites, plus up to $2.7B reasonably possible**, concentrated at Diamond Alkali/Passaic, where the Maxus indemnity is impaired by Maxus's bankruptcy ([Q1 2023 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000079746823000032/oxy-20230331.htm)). The second large item is the Anadarko Tronox-settlement deduction case in U.S. Tax Court, still pending at 3/31/2026. If OXY loses, it must repay about **$2.3B** in tentative refund, other cash benefits and interest, and the tax must be bonded or paid within 90 days of a decision. OXY booked no tax benefit for the $881M tentative refund, so a loss is mainly a cash event rather than a P&L hit ([Q1 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026030584/oxy-20260331.htm)). Taken together, plausible cash exposure is about $2–5B, or 3–8% of market capitalization. Gulf of America decommissioning obligations, pension funding, and WES gathering commitments are unknown because the 10-K notes could not be read. The Andes/Ecuador award is settled, and the Q2 10-Q describes other accrued litigation reserves as "not material."

Putting it together, my adjusted enterprise value is about **$80B**: ~$64B equity, $7.6B net debt and $8.5B preferred, before crediting the WES stake, whose 2026 size the notes could not confirm. That compares with a Yahoo-listed EV/EBITDA of about 4.5x ([Yahoo key stats](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/OXY/key-statistics/)) and a trailing P/FCF of 11.0x, against FANG at ~6.5x and EOG at ~12.5x ([stockrow](https://stockrow.com/OXY/metrics/price-to-free-cash-flow)).

There is a clear pattern. Where outcomes depend on financial engineering and the Permian, management under-promises and over-delivers. Where they depend on new technology, it over-promises. Jackson's own earlier description of the DAC strategy as "visionary and deliberate" ([Hart Energy](https://www.hartenergy.com/exclusives/occidental-dives-headfirst-direct-air-capture-analyst-raises-doubts-207152)) now sits next to his August guidance that about $400M of low-carbon capital rolls off from 2027 and that future DAC development will be **partner-funded, not funded from internal cash flow** ([Yahoo Q2 highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/occidental-petroleum-q2-earnings-call-000352360.html)). That is a de-emphasis presented as continuity. It caps downside, and it also caps OXY's share of any upside. The supporting pieces are real: EPA Class VI permits ([1PointFive](https://www.1pointfive.com/news/occidental-and-1pointfive-secure-class-vi-permits-for-stratos-direct-air-capture-facility)), DOE's April 2026 retention of the South Texas hub, though apparently with only ~$50M obligated against the $500–650M headline ([CATF](https://www.catf.us/2026/05/continued-uncertainty-department-energy-circulates-latest-retain-modify-awards-list/)), and offtakes from Microsoft (500 kt) and Amazon (250 kt) at undisclosed prices ([Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/microsoft-500000-ton-carbon-removal-purchase-1pointfive/)). None of that substitutes for tonnes captured. At its 0.5 Mt/yr design rate, Stratos's maximum 45Q value is about $90M a year at $180/t on my arithmetic. That is small relative to a ~$1.1B Carbon Engineering purchase that could face impairment if the 2027 ramp fails. The policy picture is more favorable than the DAC picture. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised 45Q for EOR to **$85/t, at parity with storage**, and Enverus estimates this cuts conventional CO2-EOR breakevens by more than 40% ([Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/newsroom/making-enhanced-oil-recovery-great-again-obba-45q-tax-credit-shift-cuts-ep-costs-by-40)). OXY, as the largest U.S. CO2-EOR operator, benefits from that through its legacy business.

Governance signals are mixed. Say-on-pay passed with **94.34%** in 2026, and support has been above 94% every year since 2021. Hollub's 2025 pay was $18.06M, at a CEO pay ratio of 80:1 ([2026 DEF 14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000162828026019816/oxy-20260319.htm); [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000797468/000095015726000569/form8-k.htm)). With Berkshire voting about 27% of the shares, those votes say little about how other holders feel. The 2026 ballot had no shareholder proposals, after As You Sow pushed in 2025 for disclosure on DAC credit double-counting ([As You Sow](https://www.asyousow.org/resolutions/2024/11/21-oxy-disclose-carbon-offset-accounting)). The structural concern is that the former CEO who designed the DAC strategy now sits on the board overseeing her successor's retreat from it. The notes could not confirm the full board roster or who the lead independent director is.

Insider behavior points one way and Berkshire's points another. No insider has sold on the open market in 2026. Director Klesse bought about $195K at $38.98 in December 2025, and Jackson bought about $250K at $52.38 on 2026-06-23 ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/4074838/insider-buying-william-klesse-acquires-shares-in-occidental-petroleum-corp); [StockTitan Form 4](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/OXY/form-4-occidental-petroleum-corp-de-insider-trading-activity-7f1c662e7be5.html)). Berkshire's share count has stayed at **264,941,431 for five quarters**. It did not add even when its 13F values implied a price near $49 at the end of June ([insiderset](https://www.insiderset.com/investor/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway/holdings)). Instead it deployed $9.5B by buying OxyChem. Under Greg Abel, Berkshire is acting like a strategic creditor and counterparty, not a buyer working toward full ownership. Internal evidence is thin: Glassdoor reviewers describe Oxy as stable with few layoffs but slow to promote ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Occidental-Petroleum-layoffs-Reviews-EI_IE491.0,20_KH21,28.htm)). A March 2026 claim of about 1,200 layoffs is unverified, and the research found no 2025–2026 EPA, OSHA or PHMSA penalties.

## The market prices about $70 oil and a wait until 2029

At $64, OXY trades near the top of its 52-week range ($38.80–$67.45). The consensus is Hold, with an average target of **$67.46** from 27 analysts ([dailypolitical/MarketBeat](https://www.dailypolitical.com/2026/10/28/occidental-petroleum-oxy-research-analysts-weekly-ratings-changes.html)). Momentum among analysts is positive: Evercore made a double upgrade in July, Goldman upgraded to Buy at $69 on 2026-10-01, Roth upgraded to Buy at $70 on 10-16, and Wells Fargo has the Street-high target of $82. Short interest is only about 2–4.6% of float ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/ss/us/oxy)). The macro backdrop drives the debate. WTI is about $97–98 and Brent $104–110, but the curve is steeply backwardated, with Dec-2027 WTI near **$81** ([commodity-board](https://commodity-board.com/crude-oil-eases-from-war-highs-but-futures-curve-signals-tight-years-ahead)). The EIA's October outlook has Brent falling from $96 in 2026 to $84 in 2027 ([EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf)).

To see what $64 implies, I ran a rough calculation. It is my own arithmetic and depends on a sensitivity of about $260M of cash flow per $1/bbl ([Market Spectator, unverified](https://www.marketspectator.com/2026/09/02/oil-hits-90-again-occidental-is-the-stock-built-for-this-moment/)), which should be checked against OXY's guidance tables. Annualizing Q2's ~$3.0B FCF gives about $12B at ~$97 oil. Adjusting linearly gives about $7.6B at $80 and about $3.7B at $65. Adding the guided $0.7–0.8B 2027 improvement and subtracting the ~$0.68B preferred dividend leaves FCF to common of roughly **$7.7B (12% yield) at $80 and $3.8B (6%) at $65**. Two things make this more optimistic than reality. It does not normalize the record Midstream & Marketing earnings, and a linear sensitivity is crude. Even so, the stock's 11x trailing P/FCF and Hold consensus fit a market that values OXY on about $70 long-run WTI, below the futures strip, and that assumes cash returns wait for the preferred call.

### Bull variant: the capital-return pivot arrives early, and self-help does not depend on oil

The market treats the preferred as a fixed overhang until 2029. In practice, OXY can reach $10B of debt with cash already on hand, and at current prices it can build the ~$8.9B call fund in about two to three years. Warrant exercise, about $5.0B if Berkshire exercises, could cover more than half of it. Retiring an 8% claim that costs more than all the bonds combined adds about $680M a year to common holders. That is the most accretive use of cash available to OXY, and it is not a delay to shareholder returns. On top of that comes self-help the company itself frames at **>$4B of added annual sustainable cash flow by 2030 at ~$65 WTI**, about half of it in 2026–27 from interest savings and capex roll-off ([Investing.com slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/occidental-q2-2026-slides-17b-debt-cut-4b-cash-flow-target-by-2030-93CH-4843861)). Collar drag of $250M in Q2–Q3 also disappears in 2027. An S&P upgrade to investment grade, 45Q-improved EOR economics, and a low-decline conventional base that justifies a lower sustaining-capex multiple than short-cycle shale peers all add to the case.

### Bear variant: 2026 is a peak, and the tails are under-counted

Trailing metrics flatter OXY. One source shows a 21% FCF yield ([Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/oxy-asset-snapshot)) and another 8.7% ([FinanceCharts](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/OXY/value/fcf-yield)). The difference is whether wartime realizations are included. Record M&M results came from crude and product dislocations that will fade. The mid-April ceasefire knocked OXY down about 10% ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/occidental-petroleum-stock-has-pulled-back-10-from-its-2026-high)), a preview of what a lasting US-Iran deal and the return of OPEC+ barrels now pumping below quota could do. Without OxyChem and without hedges, OXY enters 2027 with its highest commodity beta in years. Its Middle East assets (Al Hosn, Oman, Dolphin) and the GoA curtailment add operating exposure that pure Permian peers do not carry. Up to about $5B of tail liabilities (the Tax Court case, Passaic, guarantees of OxyChem indemnities) can arrive as lump-sum cash calls. A flat-volume company with 8.7 years of proved reserves also needs acquisitions eventually, and OXY's record on large acquisitions is Anadarko.

**My weighting.** The bear case is right that trailing numbers overstate normalized earnings. The bull case is right that the capital structure now does more for the equity than the market credits, because the cost of capital falls mechanically as the preferred and debt are retired. At about $70 long-run WTI the two roughly offset, which is why $64 looks fair-to-modestly-cheap rather than deeply undervalued. The asymmetry favors owning OXY because the self-help is contractual or already in progress, while the bear case needs a specific oil outcome. That holds only if management keeps its discipline after reaching $10B.

## Direct air capture: about $3.6B in, and the plan is to stop paying

**Assumptions added 2026-11-03 at the reader's direction.** This section treats two figures as working assumptions. Neither has been verified against filings:

- **Stratos gross capex:** $2.0–2.1B. That is well above the ~$1.2–1.3B cited in the press (see the conflict table above).
- **Holocene purchase price:** about $0.5B. OXY bought Holocene, a Knoxville DAC startup, through Oxy Low Carbon Ventures in April 2025 and did not disclose the price ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/17/carbon-removal-startup-holocene-bought-by-oil-and-gas-giant-occidental); [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/occidental-buys-holocene-marking-its-second-dac-technology-purchase/)). Holocene's pilot captured only about 10 t/yr, so $0.5B may be generous. A lower true price reduces the program total below. It does not change the Stratos economics.

### What OXY has put into DAC

| Item | Gross | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Stratos (Ector County, 0.5 Mt/yr design) | $2.0–2.1B | Assumption. BlackRock committed $550M to the JV ([1PointFive](https://www.1pointfive.com/news/occidental-and-blackrock-form-joint-venture-to-develop-stratos)). |
| Carbon Engineering acquisition (Aug 2023) | ~$1.1B | Mostly goodwill and intangibles |
| Holocene acquisition (Apr 2025) | ~$0.5B | Assumption; price undisclosed |
| **Total program** | **~$3.6–3.7B** | |
| **Net of BlackRock's $550M** | **~$3.05–3.15B** | About 5% of OXY's ~$64B market cap |

### Stratos unit economics at $2.0–2.1B

These are my estimates, not company figures. The inputs are:

- Capacity of 0.5 Mt/yr.
- Operating and maintenance cost of 3% of capex a year.
- Energy at about $50/t, using cheap Permian gas.
- The 45Q DAC credit at $180/t, which lasts 12 years ([low-carbon notes](../research_notes/Occidental%20Petroleum%20deep%20dive/low_carbon_policy_legal.md)).

The output is the carbon-removal credit price needed just to earn the cost of capital:

| Scenario | All-in cost/t | Required CDR price after $180 45Q |
|---|---|---|
| 8% cost of capital, 25-yr life, 100% utilization | $545–570 | $365–390/t |
| 10% cost of capital, 20-yr life, 100% utilization | $640–670 | $460–490/t |
| 8% cost of capital, 25-yr life, 70% utilization | $755–790 | $575–610/t |
| 10% cost of capital, 20-yr life, 70% utilization | $895–935 | $715–755/t |

At full capacity, a $2.05B project would earn roughly the following, with 45Q stopping after year 12:

| CDR price | Annual cash flow | Project return over 25 years |
|---|---|---|
| $300/t | ~$155M | ~3% |
| $450/t | ~$230M | ~9% |
| $600/t | ~$305M | ~13% |

At 70% utilization, annual cash flow falls to about $90–195M.

OXY discloses none of its credit prices. Market quotes put DAC credits at around $600/t. Microsoft has contracted 500 kt, and Amazon, JPMorganChase, Bain, Airbus, AT&T and others have smaller deals. Once Stratos runs, it should be cash-positive on an operating basis even at $300/t. It earns its cost of capital only at roughly $450/t or more and near-design uptime. Those two conditions mean the program will not repeat at this cost.

### What management will most likely do

**1. Stratos stays a one-off showcase. There will be no second plant on Carbon Engineering's design.**

- Stratos cost about $4,000–4,200 per tonne of annual capacity.
- The internal benchmark is now Holocene, which sold Google 100 kt of removal credits at **$100/t** for delivery in the early 2030s before OXY bought it ([ESG News](https://esgnews.com/oxy-buys-dac-startup-holocene-following-100-ton-carbon-removal-deal-with-google/)).
- Any future plant, whether the South Texas/King Ranch hub or a licensed project, would likely use Holocene's lower-temperature process.

**2. DAC is funded by partners and earns OXY licensing fees. OXY's own balance sheet stays out of it.**

- Jackson guided low-carbon capex down by $200–400M a year from 2027 and said future DAC will be partner-funded.
- In 2023, when he ran carbon management, he said demand for credits would push the model toward licensing ([Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/occidental-ceo-sees-potential-to-license-1000-direct-air-capture-plants/)).
- The candidate partners are BlackRock, ADNOC's XRG (up to $500M under evaluation, not yet binding), infrastructure funds and large credit buyers.

**3. An impairment is more likely than a sale in the near term.**

- The ~$1.6B of acquired technology is mostly goodwill and intangibles.
- A capex overrun of this size, plus a shift toward Holocene's technology, weakens the case for Carbon Engineering's carrying value.
- I treat a write-down in the FY2026 10-K or during 2027 as probable. It would be non-cash, but it would confirm a change of strategy.
- Selling 1PointFive outright now would lock in a large loss while Hollub, who designed the strategy, still sits on the board. A partial sale or spin becomes more plausible after the plant has a year or two of operating data, or after the board changes.

**4. The CO2 business that matters is EOR, not DAC.**

- The 2025 tax law (OBBBA) raised 45Q for CO2 used in enhanced oil recovery (EOR) from $60/t to $85/t, matching the credit for storage.
- Enverus estimates that cuts CO2-EOR breakevens from about $28/bbl to about $16/bbl.
- OXY is the largest CO2 injector in the Permian. Its real low-carbon value is cheap point-source CO2 fed into its own floods.
- DAC becomes a supplementary CO2 source and a marketing tool ("net-zero oil").

**What this means for the stock.** The market gives DAC roughly zero value, and so should investors. The upside is the *cash that stops being spent*: each $200–400M of capex that rolls off adds about 0.3–0.6% of market cap to annual free cash flow. The downside is OXY sending more good money after bad, most visibly by funding a South Texas final investment decision with its own cash.

**Credibility.** If $2.0–2.1B is right, Stratos is about 55–60% over the commonly cited ~$1.3B and roughly two years late. That reinforces the pattern described in the management section: OXY delivers on debt and on the Permian, but not on new technology.

## M&A: OXY is not a prime target, and ConocoPhillips is an unlikely buyer

A whole-company takeover should be treated as a free option, not part of the thesis. If a buyer does appear, Berkshire Hathaway is the most likely one.

### Structural obstacles to a sale of OXY

**Berkshire effectively holds a veto.**

- It owns about 27% of the common, about $8.5B of the 8% preferred, and warrants on 83.9M shares at $59.59.
- FERC cleared it in 2022 to own up to 50%.
- Buffett repeatedly said Berkshire would not take control ([Motley Fool, 2023](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/05/08/warren-buffett-isnt-acquiring-occidental-petroleum/)).
- Greg Abel, CEO since January 2026, has tilted Berkshire toward buying whole companies, for example Taylor Morrison for $6.8B ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/03/greg-abel-is-spending-berkshire-hathaways-cash-on/)). Berkshire also already owns OxyChem.
- Against that, its stake has been flat for five quarters, and it did not buy near $49.

**The deal would be very large.**

- The roughly $64B equity, ~$7.6B net debt and $8.5B preferred add up to about $80B of enterprise value.
- With a 20–30% premium, a deal would cost about $95–100B. That is effectively a stock-for-stock merger of equals for anyone except Exxon or Chevron.

**The tail liabilities would deter buyers.**

- Retained OxyChem environmental exposure: ~$1B accrued, plus up to $2.7B reasonably possible, using 2023 figures.
- OXY's guarantee of OxyChem's indemnities to Berkshire.
- The ~$2.3B Anadarko Tax Court case.
- Unknown Gulf of America decommissioning obligations.

**Timing works against a buyer.** The shares sit near their 52-week high of ~$67 on wartime oil prices, and acquirers prefer to buy at the trough. One gap remains: the change-of-control terms of the preferred, the warrants and the debt were not verified. Check the certificate of designations.

A claim on thelayoff.com that "Oxy will not exist beyond 2028" ([thread](https://www.thelayoff.com/t/1kwkynfhc?reply=%4016k+1kwkynfhc+)) is anonymous and unverified. Sure Dividend's May 2026 note judged OXY nearly fully valued and saw no acquisition at current prices ([Sure Dividend](https://www.suredividend.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/OXY-2026-05-20.pdf)).

### Why ConocoPhillips specifically is unlikely

**It has said its big acquisitions are done.**

- On 2026-02-05, Ryan Lance said "we've done our heavy lifting on the M&A side" and that the portfolio has no strategic gaps ([OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/companies/news/55355729/conocophillips-ceo-on-mergers-and-acquisitions-been-there-done-that)).
- COP only just finished integrating Marathon Oil, with more than $1B of run-rate synergies ([COP Q4 2025](https://www.conocophillips.com/news-media/story/conocophillips-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-announces-2026-guidance-and-quarterly-dividend/)).
- Lance told staff that absorbing acquisitions had made COP less competitive, and he announced cuts of up to 25% of the workforce ([Boursorama/Reuters](https://www.boursorama.com/bourse/actualites/le-directeur-general-de-conoco-declare-aux-employes-confrontes-a-des-suppressions-d-emplois-importantes-je-me-reproche-de-ne-pas-avoir-ete-plus-attentif-4121c400b23978ad99cfc2c6f72de368)).

**Its priorities point the other way.** COP is selling assets, having hit a $5B disposition target early. It is returning about 45% of cash from operations to shareholders and has cut capex to about $12B ([COP Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001163165/000116316526000030/cop-20260806x8kexx991.htm)).

**Much of OXY would not fit COP.**

- The Permian would fit. CrownRock's Midland acreage sits next to Concho's, and the two companies' Delaware acreage overlaps.
- COP exited the Gulf of Mexico years ago, and it would gain little from the CO2-EOR franchise, DAC, the WES stake or the OxyChem legacy liabilities.
- COP would be paying an $80B-plus price for roughly half a company it wants.

**One wildcard.** Andy O'Brien became COP's CEO on 2026-09-01 and previously ran its M&A ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/conocophillips-ceo-ryan-lance-departs-as-oil-producer-posts-best-profit-since-2022.html)). He has not yet stated an M&A stance. COP reports Q3 on 2026-11-05.

### What is more likely

Asset-level transactions are the more likely outcome. Candidates include a sale of Gulf of America or international assets, a partial or full sale of the WES stake, and a partial sale of 1PointFive. A new CEO, a repaired balance sheet and flat-production guidance all fit a company simplifying itself rather than selling itself.

**Signals that would raise the odds of a whole-company deal:**

- A Berkshire HSR filing, renewed open-market buying in the ~11-14 13F, or exercise of its warrants.
- Abel discussing OXY in his annual letter or at the 2027 meeting.
- OXY divesting the Gulf of America or international assets, or ring-fencing its legacy liabilities.
- O'Brien reopening large-scale M&A at COP.
- A Permian consolidation wave, for example FANG, DVN or EOG being acquired.

## Conclusion

OXY's central question has changed. Through 2025 it was whether a leveraged company could survive the Anadarko and CrownRock debt. Now it is whether a deleveraged company will hand the benefit to common shareholders before oil normalizes. The less obvious point is that the preferred, usually framed as a cap on buybacks, is also OXY's best investment: retiring it earns a guaranteed 8% (about 7.6% after the 105% call premium), and the in-the-money warrants can fund much of it. I hold a constructive, conditional view: a modest buy below about $65, with fair value around $70–75 if long-dated WTI holds about $75 and the 2027 self-help arrives as guided, and downside toward the mid-$40s, roughly where the shares traded in June and where insiders bought, if the back of the curve breaks below $65. The carbon business should be valued at near zero. With about $3.6–3.7B sunk on the working assumptions (Stratos at $2.0–2.1B, plus Carbon Engineering and Holocene), a write-down of the acquired technology is more likely than upside, and the real gain for shareholders is the capex that stops from 2027. A whole-company takeover is a free option, not part of the thesis. Berkshire, not ConocoPhillips, is the only plausible buyer. Management's credibility in the Permian and on the balance sheet has been earned. Its credibility on new technology has not, and the board seat of the strategy's architect is the governance item to watch as Jackson reshapes it.
